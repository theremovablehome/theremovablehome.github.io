Who writes this

About

A small independent site about making a rental feel like yours without treating the walls as if you own them.

The Removable Home is written by Avery Quinn.

Avery rents, like a lot of people who search for “no-drill curtain rods” at 10 p.m. after moving into a place with builder-beige walls and blinds that don’t close. This site exists because most home-upgrade advice assumes you can drive a screw into a stud, paint whatever you want, and leave it there. Renters cannot do that — or they can, and then they pay for it when they move out.

The goal here is narrower than “home decor.” Every guide starts with three questions:

  1. Will this damage paint, tile, drywall, or window frames?
  2. Can a careful person reverse it in an afternoon?
  3. Does the product’s marketing overpromise what “removable” means on a real wall?

How guides are researched

These are practical buying and how-to guides, not lab reviews. Avery compares manufacturer specs (weight ratings, heat limits, adhesive type, removal instructions), looks at the failure modes that show up again and again (fresh paint lifting, textured walls rejecting film, single-hose portable ACs fighting themselves), and writes the steps a renter actually needs: measuring, wall-type checks, cure times, and a removal plan.

If a method leaves even a small hole — tap-in curtain brackets are the usual example — the guide says so. “Renter-friendly” is not the same thing as “invisible.”

Nothing on this site is legal advice, and nothing here is a promise that your landlord, property manager, or lease will allow a particular product. Leases differ. When a rule is unclear, ask in writing before you buy.

What this site is not

It is not a magazine for people renovating a house they own. It is not a listing of every product in a category. It does not invent hands-on test scores for items that were not installed in a real apartment for months. When a recommendation is based on specifications and known tradeoffs rather than long-term personal use, the article says so.
